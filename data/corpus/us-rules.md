# US Market Claims Standard: Efficacy Claims

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. Invented for a reference implementation. It is not the
> standard of any real company or regulator.

## Purpose

This standard is applied by the US corporate affairs team when reviewing advertising
claims about the effects of over-the-counter health products. It applies to all
media, including television, print and social media.

## Rule 1: Efficacy claims require two independent trials

Any claim that a product has an effect must be supported by at least two independent,
randomised, controlled trials that each show the effect. Independent means designed,
conducted and analysed without involvement of the manufacturer.

A claim supported by fewer than two such trials is not approved.

## Rule 2: Claims must not overstate the evidence

A claim must not state or imply an effect that is larger than, or different in kind
from, the effect shown in the supporting trials. Wording is assessed against the
overall body of evidence, not against any single measurement.

## Rule 3: No separate timing requirement

This standard does not contain a separate requirement that a trial directly measured
the effect at the specific time point mentioned in a claim. Timing statements are
assessed under Rules 1 and 2 in the same way as any other statement about the effect.

## Reviewer guidance

- The reviewer records how many independent trials support the claim and names them.
- Where the supporting trials did not directly measure a time point mentioned in the
  claim, the reviewer notes this in the review record, even though it is not a
  ground for rejection under this standard.
