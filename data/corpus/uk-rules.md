# UK Market Claims Standard: Efficacy and Timing Claims

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. Invented for a reference implementation. It is not the
> standard of any real company or regulator.

## Purpose

This standard is applied by the UK corporate affairs team when reviewing advertising
claims about the effects of over-the-counter health products. It applies to all
media, including television, print and social media.

## Rule 1: Efficacy claims must be substantiated

Any claim that a product has an effect must be supported by evidence that is
available to the reviewer at the time of review. A claim without supporting evidence
is not approved.

## Rule 2: Timing claims require direct measurement

A timing claim is any claim that states or implies how quickly a product works, such
as "relieves pain in 15 minutes", "works within the hour" or "fast-acting relief".

A timing claim may only be approved if at least one trial **directly measured the
effect at or before the time point claimed**.

Results at later time points must not be extrapolated backwards. A trial that first
measured the effect at 30 minutes provides no support for a claim about 15 minutes,
whatever the size of the effect measured at 30 minutes.

## Rule 3: "Clinically proven" requires an independent randomised trial

The wording "clinically proven" may only be used where at least one independent,
randomised, controlled trial supports the specific claim being made. Independent
means designed, conducted and analysed without involvement of the manufacturer.

## Reviewer guidance

- Where a timing claim fails Rule 2 but the evidence supports a later time point, the
  recommendation should name the earliest measured time point that was supported and
  suggest rewording the claim to that time point.
- Each rule is assessed separately, and the reviewer records which rules were met and
  which were not.
