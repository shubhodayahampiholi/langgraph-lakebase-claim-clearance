# India Market Claims Standard: Efficacy and "Clinically Proven" Claims

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. Invented for a reference implementation. It is not the
> standard of any real company or regulator.

## Purpose

This standard is applied by the India corporate affairs team when reviewing
advertising claims about the effects of over-the-counter health products. It applies
to all media, including television, print and social media.

## Rule 1: Efficacy claims require at least one randomised trial

Any claim that a product has an effect must be supported by at least one randomised,
controlled trial that shows the effect. A claim without such support is not approved.

## Rule 2: "Clinically proven" requires local-population evidence

The wording "clinically proven" may only be used where at least one randomised,
controlled trial supporting the claim enrolled a cohort from the local population
(participants recruited in India), subject to all of the following:

- The local cohort contains at least 50 participants.
- The results for the local cohort are reported separately from the combined results.
- The local cohort results are consistent in direction with the combined results.

Evidence from trials conducted only outside India does not satisfy this rule,
whatever its size or quality.

## Rule 3: No separate timing requirement

This standard does not contain a separate requirement that a trial directly measured
the effect at the specific time point mentioned in a claim.

## Reviewer guidance

- The reviewer records which trial supplies the local-population evidence, the size
  of the local cohort, and whether its results are reported separately.
- Where no local-population evidence exists, the recommendation should say so and
  suggest wording that does not use "clinically proven".
